### Some Hypotheses:

1. I believe that being a generalist is important to compress ownership, understanding of product, produces versatility of framing, challenges the way I think, and reduces communication tax.
2. Problem framing is paramount both by perspective and by [Heilmeier's Catechisms](https://www.darpa.mil/about/heilmeier-catechism).
3. I believe "Simple Solutions to Complex Problems" are provably better: according to Occam's Razor/ [Minimal Description Length (Information Theory)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimum_description_length) / [Solomonoff's theory of inductive inference
](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomonoff%27s_theory_of_inductive_inference); in fact, Josh from the Browser Company cites Mark Zuckerberg saying this in [Five Companies Inspiring Our Work Now](https://open.spotify.com/episode/6tgN9JH5p5k9mKn4OZcHP8?si=820e838a6a4d4681), also [Eugene Yan in-defense of Simplicity](https://eugeneyan.com/writing/simplicity/). Very simply, two programs which output the same thing for every input, the shorter one has less arbitrary constraints, ones not grounded by observation and potentially erroneous. Although this requires problem framing ability and rigorous understanding of the problem at hand. Caveat that perfection isn't computationally tractable, but best effort is always possible. I heard "Good is Good Enough" from my friend's time in Databricks.
5. Prototyping to learn is actually just focused technical learning without combinatorial constraints of business software. Both are important somewhat orthogonal signals which are important.
6. There is an invariant in the world where technology can and will always be getting better, hopefully for the better of people. This makes the existence of work/ jobs a constant, this amelioration is an infinite iterative game, in a good sense. There are many fields where better is always better like in healthcare and education.
7. Theory is the compression and formalization of observation. Compression = Faster learning. There is nothing "as too theoretical" its the problem of the observer to understand it rather than the problem of the theory itself, however, requires correct formalization and rigorous questioning. Pythagorean Theorem, Calculus, Relational Algebra, Turing Machines, Information Theory, Neural Networks, were all theories at one point now ubiquitous powering our world and lives and powers every device we own. Our negative perception comes from the connatation and out of context mis-application, the existence of erroroneus use is orthogonal to the structural generalization it offers.
8. I also believe good things happen when we keep trying.
